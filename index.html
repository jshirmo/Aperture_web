Aperture

A little more clarity. A lot more possibility.

Meet Aperture: an AI-powered visual companion that helps people who are blind or have low vision read text, find objects, and navigate their surroundings—all through a camera.

One companion. Three ways to help.

Everyday moments, made easier.

A label you want to read. An object you need to find. A room you want to understand. Aperture turns visual information into spoken feedback, helping you take on the moment with more confidence.

TOOL 01

Read It

Point your camera at printed text and let Aperture bring the words to you. Hear a summary or have the text read aloud, then adjust the speed or listen again.

FOOD PACKAGES · LABELS · SIGNS · MAIL
TOOL 02

Find It

Looking for something? Tell Aperture what you need and listen for its approximate location, described through familiar clock-face directions and distance.

“AT 2 O’CLOCK, ABOUT 7 FEET AWAY.”
TOOL 03

What Am I Looking At?

Get a clearer picture of the space ahead. Aperture describes visible objects and text, and can point out possible obstacles in view.

Why Aperture matters

More independence in everyday life.

Finding something that has fallen or getting to know an unfamiliar room can take time and effort. Aperture was created to make visual information more accessible in moments like these, supporting independence in everyday life.

“If I drop my medicine, it’s like it’s gone into a black hole on the white tile floor.”— Participant in an at-home assistive technology study
Accessibility from the start

Designed to be heard, seen, and used.

Aperture offers different ways to navigate and receive information, so people can use the tools in the way that works for them.

Screen-reader support

Announcements communicate changes and status as you navigate.

Easy to see

High-contrast themes and adjustable text sizes make the interface easier to see.

Easy to reach

Large, mobile-friendly controls make it easier to move between tasks.

Spoken feedback

Listen to information aloud and hear it again when you need it.

Meet the creator

The Story Behind Aperture

Hi, I’m Jasmine Shirmohammad, a neuroscience student at Tulane University pursuing minors in Spanish and AI literacy. My interests in research, medicine, communication, and community service shape the way I approach new ideas.

I’ve always been drawn to work that helps me understand people and find meaningful ways to support them. Whether I’m studying neuroscience, volunteering in my community, or learning how to communicate with future patients in their own language, I’m motivated by the same question: How can I use what I’m learning to make someone else’s day a little easier?

That question helped inspire Aperture.

I began thinking about the visual information many of us encounter throughout the day: the words on a medicine label, an object that has fallen out of reach, or the layout of an unfamiliar room. For someone who is blind or has low vision, accessing that information can require extra time, effort, or assistance. I wanted to explore how technology could help make those everyday moments more accessible.

Aperture brings together my interest in neuroscience, artificial intelligence, and service in one project. Using a phone’s camera and AI, it helps users read text, locate objects, and learn more about their surroundings through spoken feedback. I designed it around a simple idea: the information someone needs should be easier to access when they need it.

Creating Aperture has helped me see how technology can be a form of service. An app alone cannot remove every barrier, and building something truly useful requires listening to the people who may use it. But I believe thoughtful ideas can be a starting point for greater accessibility, independence, and connection.

My hope for Aperture is the same hope that guides my path toward medicine: to use curiosity, compassion, and what I continue to learn to serve my community and help people feel supported in their everyday lives.
